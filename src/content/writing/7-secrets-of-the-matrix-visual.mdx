Some of you Excel users may have realised that the Power BI Matrix visual is just an Excel Pivot Table by another term.  You even get buckets for "Rows", "Columns" and "Values" just like constructing an Excel Pivot Table.

The temptation is to think this is all the Matrix visual can do; act like, well, a typical Pivot Table: -

<Screenshot src="7-secrets-of-the-matrix-visual/01-matrix-and-pivot-table-dance-scores.png" alt="A Power BI matrix titled Dance Scores by Week beside an Excel PivotTable showing the same data: contestants in rows, weeks 1 to 13 in columns, each contestant's weekly score, and totals for each contestant and each week. The grand total is 3797 in both." />

You can see that both the Matrix and the Pivot Table have the Contestant field in "Rows" and the Week field in "Columns", showing Contestants' dance scores (in "Values") for each week, along with the grand total for each Contestant and each Week.

<Screenshot src="7-secrets-of-the-matrix-visual/02-matrix-and-pivot-table-field-wells.png" alt="The matrix's field wells beside the PivotTable's field areas: Contestant in Rows, Week in Columns, and Scores (Sum of Scores in Excel) in Values." />

However, depending on your knowledge of Excel Pivot tables, you'll know that if the Matrix is just like an Excel Pivot Table, then you should be able to do a lot more than just show a "matrix" of data.    The trouble is that unlike the Excel Pivot Table, where all the options are readily at hand on the Excel Ribbon, most of the Matrix options are so hidden away they could be described as "secrets".

With this in mind, here are my seven favourite Matrix secrets.

## Secret No.1 - Grand Totals are really Subtotals.

In an Excel Pivot Table, grand totals are easily removed. Just use the button on the Ribbon labelled "Grand Totals".

<Screenshot src="7-secrets-of-the-matrix-visual/03-excel-grand-totals-menu.png" alt="The Grand Totals button on Excel's ribbon, open to show Off for Rows and Columns, On for Rows and Columns, On for Rows Only and On for Columns Only." />

In a Power BI Matrix, you might think you'd be able to do the same thing on the Column grand totals or Row grand totals cards, but you won't see any option to turn them off.  You just get options to format the grand totals.

<Screenshot src="7-secrets-of-the-matrix-visual/04-column-grand-total-card.png" alt="The Column grand total card in the format pane, with font, bold, text colour, background colour and Apply to labels settings, and no setting to turn the total off." />

This is what I mean by "secretive". Where is the option to turn off the grand totals hidden?

Well, one thing you must understand is that in a Power BI Matrix, grand totals are really subtotals!  So, to turn grand totals off, turn off the Column or Row subtotals.

<Screenshot src="7-secrets-of-the-matrix-visual/05-subtotals-toggles-off.png" alt="The Column subtotals and Row subtotals cards in the format pane, both switched off." />

<Screenshot src="7-secrets-of-the-matrix-visual/06-matrix-without-grand-totals.png" alt="The Dance Scores by Week matrix with no total row and no total column." />

However, be warned; if you have multiple fields in the Rows bucket, this will also remove all your subtotals, not just the grand total.  To remove only the grand total while retaining other subtotals, you'll need to uncover another secret, see Secret No.4 below.

## Secret No.2 - Expanding all down one level in the hierarchy.

The starting point to understanding this secret is to appreciate that it's not until you work with multiple fields in the "Rows" bucket that your Matrix can become more informative.  You'll see on the right that I now have both Contestant and Professional in Rows.

<Screenshot src="7-secrets-of-the-matrix-visual/07-rows-contestant-and-professional.png" alt="The Rows field well holding Contestant and then Professional." />

Unfortunately, when you put a second field into Rows, it may appear that nothing much has happened; secretive or what!  However, you'll notice a series of arrows at the top right (or bottom right) of the visual and there is one label "Expand all down one level in the hierarchy".  Apparently, this is what you need to click on to see the second row.

<Screenshot src="7-secrets-of-the-matrix-visual/08-expand-all-down-one-level.png" alt="The icons above the matrix, with the fork-shaped icon outlined in red and its tooltip reading Expand all down one level in the hierarchy." />

<Screenshot src="7-secrets-of-the-matrix-visual/09-matrix-indented-with-subtotals.png" alt="The expanded matrix: each contestant's name in bold with their scores, and their professional partner's name indented on the row below with the same scores." />

Looking at the Matrix now however, it's not exactly what I want.  The Professional's names are indented ***under*** each Contestant's name when they really should be sitting beside each other, and because there is only one Professional for each Contestant, I get irrelevant subtotals on the Contestant rows (in bold).  More secrets need revealing!

## Secret No.3 – Turn off "Stepped layout".

With most Matrices, the indented or "Stepped" layout gives less clarity to which rows the values refer to (this is the equivalent to "Compact layout" of the Excel Pivot Table). It's better to put each Row label in a separate column. To do this, just turn off "Stepped layout" on the Row headers card.

<Screenshot src="7-secrets-of-the-matrix-visual/10-stepped-layout-off.png" alt="The format pane's Row headers card, Options section, with the Stepped layout switch turned off and outlined in red. In the matrix, Contestant and Professional are now in separate columns, with a bold Total row under each contestant." />

<UpdateNote>

**Update, November 2026:** Power BI no longer has a Stepped layout switch. In the format pane, go to Layout, then Matrix layout, and choose Tabular to put each row field in its own column with no indentation. Compact is the indented layout, and Outline also gives each field its own column but shows subtotals at the top of each group.

</UpdateNote>

This is definitely an improvement, but I still see those subtotals and they've now moved to a row of their own, labelled "Total".  I can turn off the Row subtotals on the Row subtotals card (as explained in Secret No. 1 above) and now I just have subtotals for each Contestant, not each week.

<Screenshot src="7-secrets-of-the-matrix-visual/11-matrix-side-by-side-no-subtotals.png" alt="The matrix with Contestant and Professional side by side, no subtotal rows, and a bold Total column on the right giving each contestant's total score." />

## Secret No.4 - Only show the subtotals/grand totals you want to see.

However, if I add a third field to the Rows bucket e.g. for the Judges (I also need to expand down all one level in the hierarchy again) and turn Row subtotals on again, things become a little more challenging.  I get subtotals for ***both*** Professionals and Judges, showing the same values, as well as grand totals for all the Contestants for each week at the very bottom of the matrix.

<Screenshot src="7-secrets-of-the-matrix-visual/12-rows-with-judge.png" alt="The Rows field well holding Contestant, Professional and Judge." />

<Screenshot src="7-secrets-of-the-matrix-visual/13-row-subtotals-on-three-levels.png" alt="The matrix with a Judge column showing each judge's scores, and two identical bold Total rows under each contestant. In the format pane, the Row subtotals switch is on and outlined in red, with Per row level off and Series set to All." />

Before I control which subtotals show however, I can take the opportunity of formatting the subtotals by using the "Values" card.  Don't forget that if you want the grand totals formatted differently from the subtotals, use the Row grand totals card as shown in Secret No.1 above.

<Screenshot src="7-secrets-of-the-matrix-visual/14-subtotals-values-formatting.png" alt="The Values card under Row subtotals with a magenta text colour and Apply to labels on. In the matrix, the subtotal rows and their Total labels are magenta." />

I want to remove the subtotals only for the Professionals.  So how do I control which subtotals and/or grand totals to show? This secret is really hidden away where no one can find it!

On the Row subtotals card, turn on "Apply settings to Per Row Level".  By default, subtotals show for all series, so you need to select the Series whose subtotals you want to ***remove.*** Then on the Rows card, turn the subtotals off for that series.  I want to remove the subtotals for the Professional series.

<Screenshot src="7-secrets-of-the-matrix-visual/15-per-row-level-professional-off.png" alt="The Row subtotals card with Per row level on, Series set to Professional and, on the Rows card, Show subtotal off, each outlined in red. The matrix now has one Total row under each contestant." />

<UpdateNote>

**Update, November 2026:** Power BI now labels this dropdown Row level, where the text and screenshots say Series. It is under Row subtotals, Apply settings to, Per row level.

</UpdateNote>

Note that if you want to remove grand totals, these are the subtotals associated with the first field listed in the Series dropdown e.g. Contestant, in my case.

Also note that the Values card is enabled only if "All" is selected in the Series dropdown.  You don't seem to be able to format different subtotals separately (except for the grand totals).  You can click on "Revert to default" at the bottom of the Row subtotals card to enable the Values card.

## Secret No.5 – Show Values on Rows

Ever wanted to do this?  Compare the two Matrix visuals below.  On the left, the Values are in columns, which is the default.  On the right, the values have been placed in the Rows.  This is a well-known secret, but you can use the Values card (under the Grid card) and then expand the Options card, turning on "Switch values to rows"

<Screenshot src="7-secrets-of-the-matrix-visual/16-switch-values-to-rows.png" alt="Two matrices of the same data. On the left, Scores, Avg Dance Score and Avg Score are columns. On the right, they are listed in rows beside each contestant. The format pane shows Values, Options, with Switch values to rows on and outlined in red." />

## Secret No.6 – Show text in the Values area

Ever wanted to put the names or descriptions into the values area of the Matrix, as in the example below.  You can see that in Rows is Contestant name, in Columns is the Week and the Values area is populated with the names of the dances performed.

<Screenshot src="7-secrets-of-the-matrix-visual/17-dance-names-in-values.png" alt="A matrix with contestants in rows and weeks in columns, where each cell holds the name of a dance, such as Cha Cha Cha, Quickstep or Rumba, instead of a number." />

In the Values bucket of the Matrix, I have placed a DAX measure, "Dance Name"

<Screenshot src="7-secrets-of-the-matrix-visual/18-dance-name-measure-in-values.png" alt="The field wells: Contestant in Rows, Week in Columns and the Dance Name measure in Values." />

This is the DAX for the "Dance Name" measure: -

```dax
Dance Name = SELECTEDVALUE(Dances[Dance])
```

In the brackets of the SELECTEDVALUE function, put the column name of the column you want to put in the middle of your Matrix (in our case the Dance column from the Dances table) and use this measure in the Values bucket.  However, this measure can only show values if there is only one value to show e.g. each contestant performs only one dance in each week.
